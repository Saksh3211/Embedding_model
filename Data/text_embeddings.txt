An embedding represents an item as a list of numbers called a vector.
A text embedding maps words, sentences, or documents into a vector space.
Words with similar meanings can have nearby vectors when training data supports that pattern.
A sentence encoder converts a complete sentence into one fixed length vector.
A word embedding assigns a vector to each word in its vocabulary.
The vocabulary is the collection of tokens known to the model.
A tokenizer splits raw text into smaller units before training.
A token can be a word, a subword, or a punctuation mark.
The embedding dimension is the number of values in each vector.
Cosine similarity compares the direction of two vectors.
Similar vectors can help retrieve related documents from a collection.
A search system embeds a query and compares it with document vectors.
The nearest neighbors are the vectors with the highest similarity scores.
Training examples teach a model which words and phrases appear in related contexts.
A context window contains nearby tokens around a target word.
A larger context window gives the model a wider view of surrounding text.
A corpus is the collection of text used to train a language model.
Clean and varied training text helps the model learn useful word relationships.
A small corpus is useful for learning but often produces unreliable similarities.
A vector database stores embeddings and supports fast similarity search.
Normalization scales a vector to unit length before cosine similarity is calculated.
An out of vocabulary word is a token that the model did not learn during training.
A training loss measures how well the model predicts its training examples.
A lower training loss does not always mean that search results are useful.
